## What Hayven is, in two paragraphs

Hayven is an online platform that connects Nigerian families with independent
therapists (speech, behavioural, developmental) for children, many of them autistic
or with developmental delays. Parents create an account, add their child, book and
pay for sessions; therapists record session notes and goals and publish progress
reports to the parent. The platform holds children's health and developmental data,
which makes it sensitive personal data under the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023.

The platform is built and running. We're preparing to open it to real families and
therapists, and we need your review before we do. Every document listed below is a
draft we wrote ourselves; none has been reviewed by a lawyer or a safeguarding
professional. **Please treat nothing in them as settled.**

## Already decided by Hayven, for you to check rather than decide

Therapist Agreement terms (decided 2026-10-09):

- Therapists **must hold professional indemnity insurance**, with proof at
  verification. The minimum cover is open (question C11).
- **Non-circumvention:** 12 months after the last platform session with a family.
- **Notice:** 30 days before Hayven ends the agreement without cause, and 30 days
  before any commission change.
- **Liability cap:** Hayven's liability to a therapist is capped at the commission
  it earned from them in the previous 12 months. The cap excludes earnings owed and
  fraud.
- **Acceptance:** click-to-accept at verification, with the version and time
  recorded, and re-acceptance after a material change.
- Commission is 20% to Hayven and 80% to the therapist. Earnings clear 8 days after
  a session (the 7-day window for a parent to report a problem, plus a day) and are
  paid every Monday. This is already live.

## Questions for the safeguarding adviser

**S1. The procedure as a whole (§3–§6).** Is what counts as a concern, how a
therapist responds, and how the DSL triages and decides proportionate and safe for
a small online platform where therapists work independently and often remotely?

**S2. Escalation channel (§4.3).** Concerns go to the DSL by phone (urgent) or
email the same working day, then to the Deputy DSL after an hour without a
response, and to the police on 112 if a child is in danger and neither lead
answers. Is this right? Should the timings change?

**S3. The in-app form.** A therapist logs which child the concern is about, what
they saw or heard, and whether the child may be in danger now. Is anything missing
that a safeguarding record should capture at the point of raising it?

**S4. The DSL's record (§7).** For each concern the DSL records a decision
(monitor, contact the family, refer externally, immediate escalation, or no action),
the reasoning, a review date and the outcome. Is that enough?

**S5. Automatic alerts.** These words in a published session trigger an alert to
the DSL: *parent argument, parents arguing, self-harm, self harm, suicide, abuse,
police, child protective, CPS, bruise, injury at home*. Words such as "seizure" or
"lack of sleep" only warn the therapist. Should anything be added or removed?

**S6. Concerns about a therapist (§5.3).** The DSL can end a therapist's access to
one child immediately, or suspend their whole account. Is "when in doubt, suspend"
the right default?

**S7. Training.** What minimum safeguarding training, if any, should therapists
need before they can be verified?

**S8. Parents.** At present only therapists can raise a concern in-app. Should
parents have their own route as well?

## Questions for counsel

### Safeguarding law

**C1. Mandatory reporting.** Does Nigerian law, including the Child's Rights Act
2003 and Lagos State law, impose a duty on Hayven or its therapists to report
suspected abuse? If so, to whom, and within what time?

**C2. Referral authorities (§5.2).** We found these contacts by public web search:
the Lagos DSVRT (0800 033 3333) and the Lagos State Child Protection Unit. Are they
the correct bodies for a child-welfare referral? What applies to a child outside
Lagos?

**C3. Retention of safeguarding records (§7).** We found no Nigeria-specific
retention period for child-protection records. Our placeholder is "until the child
turns 18, plus several years". What should it be?

**C4. Sharing with authorities.** If the DSL refers a concern externally, what
lawful basis under the NDPA covers sharing a child's information without the
parent's consent, especially where the parent may be the concern?

### Data protection (NDPA 2023)

**C5. Lawful basis.** Which lawful basis applies to each processing activity in the
DPIA's §3 table? The most important are the child's health data (rows 2–4) and
referrals received from a third-party clinician (row 10). Detail is in the counsel
briefing, Part A.

**C6. Registration.** Is Hayven a "data controller of major importance" that must
register with the NDPC (s.44)?

**C7. Guardian verification (s.31).** A parent currently *confirms* that they are
the child's guardian with a checkbox. Is that enough, or does the Act require us to
*verify* it? If it requires verification, what is proportionate?

**C8. International transfers.** Some providers process data outside Nigeria: the
database and servers in the UK, email and document scanning in the US. Which NDPA
transfer mechanism applies, and do the providers' standard DPAs suffice? Detail is
in the counsel briefing, Part C.

**C9. Breach notification.** Please confirm the NDPC filing mechanics and the
72-hour window in the breach runbook §6, and when we must notify affected families
(§7).

**C16. Launch waitlist.** While no therapist is live, the search page lets anyone
leave an email address to be told once when therapists are available. Does the
Privacy Policy need to say this, and on what lawful basis do we keep these addresses?

**C17. Account security.** On 9 October 2026 we removed two-factor authentication for
every account, admins included. Sign-in is now email and password (or Google, for
parents), with rate-limited login and account suspension. Is that adequate for
accounts that can read children's health data and the safeguarding log? (DPIA, update
IX and risk #1.)

### Therapist Agreement

**C10. Employment status.** Is "independent contractor" defensible, given that
Hayven sets the commission, the payout schedule and conduct standards, and controls
verification?

**C11. Insurance.** What minimum professional indemnity cover should we require?
Is such cover realistically available to Nigerian therapists?

**C12. Indemnity clause.** Please draft the clause under which a therapist
indemnifies Hayven for their own clinical conduct.

**C13. Wording of the decided terms.** Please draft or confirm the non-circumvention
clause, so it is enforceable rather than a restraint of trade, and the liability
cap. Please also recommend a process for a therapist to dispute a payment.

**C14. Disputes.** Should the agreement require mediation or arbitration before
court? Is Lagos the right venue, given that therapists may practise anywhere in
Nigeria?

**C15. Terms of Service.** The Terms need to say that therapists must be insured.
Please review the Terms and the Privacy Policy as a whole.
